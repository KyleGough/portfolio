# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Hiring managers, technical peers, and collaborators who land on the site to judge depth of work, scan projects quickly, and optionally reach out via the contact form. They are often in a review mindset: comparing candidates or verifying skills referenced elsewhere.

## Product Purpose

A personal portfolio for Kyle Gough (kylegough.co.uk) that showcases selected software projects, skills, and background. It exists to convert curiosity into trust: show real builds, not buzzwords, and make it easy to get from overview to project detail to contact. Success is credible first impressions, low friction navigation, and accurate representation of the work over time as the stack evolved (static HTML through to Next.js).

## Brand Personality

Clear, evidence-led, and quietly confident. The site should feel like an engineer-owned space: legible, fast, and structured, with room for personality (for example, the home terminal motif) without gimmicks. Tone is professional and direct, not salesy.

## Anti-references

- Generic "AI portfolio" or template lookalikes: identical card grids, meaningless hero metrics, decorative gradients.
- Dark-mode-for-cool alone or neon-on-black tech clichés without a scene that justifies them.
- Dense walls of unbroken text; unreadable case studies.
- Modals or heavy chrome where inline or progressive disclosure would serve visitors better.

## Design Principles

1. **Show the build.** Favor screenshots, live links, and concrete stack detail over abstract claims.
2. **Respect the reader’s time.** Scannable structure, clear hierarchy, and predictable navigation.
3. **Stability and craft.** The stack and CI matter; the interface should feel maintained and intentionally simple.
4. **Access over effects.** Motion and visual flair support comprehension, not decoration by default.
5. **One voice.** Accent and link colors are deliberate; avoid competing focal points on a single view.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target **WCAG 2.1 Level AA** for text contrast, focus visibility, and form labels. Self-hosted fonts use `font-display: swap` where declared. Support keyboard use for nav and contact flows; respect `prefers-reduced-motion` where motion is not essential. Adjust if you adopt stricter org or client requirements.
